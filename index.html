How it works

Three steps, and none of them is “type it again”.

The order is written once, by whoever is standing at the table — and everything else in the house reads from it.

STEP 01

The waiter takes the order

Tap the table, tap the items. One tap adds one — the way people actually order another round. Notes like “no pepper” travel with the item.

STEP 02

The kitchen gets it instantly

It lands with a sound, a banner and a timer, and the screen stays awake. Nobody has to be looking at it for the order to be noticed.

STEP 03

The bill is already right

Every round is on the same tab. Split it, take part in cash and part by card, print it — the table only clears when it is paid.

Modules

Five parts, one system.

Not five products to buy separately. It is how the operation divides up inside — and every part reads the same order.

Menu

Categories, products, photos, notes and prices. Marking something sold out is one tap, and it leaves the guest's menu immediately.

Orders

Table, counter, QR, pickup and delivery apps all land in one place, each with its own number and a history of every state change.

Kitchen

A real KDS: new, preparing, ready, delivered. A timer per ticket, an alert that keeps insisting, and a screen that does not fall asleep.

Tables

A QR per table, open and occupied states, orders per table, partial and split payment — and the table only clears when it is paid.

Payments

Card, cash and instant transfer recorded at close, reconciled by method and by channel. Card terminals plug in as connectors — see below.

Management and access

Sales reporting, roles per job, and an audit trail. The kitchen sees no prices, the floor sees no revenue, and one venue never sees another's data — enforced in the database, not by convention.

Straight answers

The questions owners actually ask.

Who enters the menu and the products?

Your choice. You can create the account and build the menu yourself in the panel — adding a product is quick, and changing a price or marking something sold out is one tap. And if you would rather type nothing, assisted setup still stands: hand over the menu exactly as it is today, even a photo of the laminated card, and our team gives the system back with products, prices, categories, tables and staff already in it.

Do I need to buy tablets or a POS terminal?

No. It runs in the browser on the phones your staff already carry. If you want a screen on the kitchen wall, the cheapest Android tablet will do — it only has to stay plugged in and on the wi-fi.

What happens if the internet drops?

Orders already sent stay on the kitchen screen. New ones need the connection, the same as your card machine does. We'll say it plainly: this is a connected system, and a dining room without reliable wi-fi is not the right room for it yet.

Do my guests have to download an app?

Never. The QR code opens the menu in the phone's browser. And if you'd rather your waiters take every order, you don't have to use the QR codes at all.

Can I take payments through it?

Today the person at the till records what came in — cash, card, instant transfer — and the tab closes with it. Card-machine integrations are added one at a time, using the provider's official tools only, never a reverse-engineered endpoint.

Who owns the data?

You do. Your menu, your sales, your guests. Each venue is isolated at the database level, and you can ask for an export at any time.
